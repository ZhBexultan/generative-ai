# Generative AI Vocabulary

1. **Generative AI (GenAI)**: A branch of artificial intelligence focused on creating new content (text, code, images, audio) based on patterns learned from massive amounts of existing data, rather than just analyzing or classifying it.
2. **LLM (Large Language Model)**: A massive neural network trained on vast amounts of text data, allowing it to understand natural language and generate human-like text responses.
3. **Prompt Engineering**: The skill of carefully formulating and structuring text instructions (prompts) to get the most accurate and useful output from an AI model.
4. **Hallucination**: A phenomenon where an AI confidently generates incorrect, nonsensical, or entirely fabricated information, presenting it as a fact.
5. **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)**: A technique that improves AI responses by first searching an external database for relevant facts, and then passing those facts to the AI to generate a grounded and accurate answer.
6. **Token**: The basic building block of text for an AI. It can be a word, part of a word, or a single character. Models have limits on how many tokens they can process at once.
7. **Context Window**: The maximum amount of text (measured in tokens) that an AI model can remember and consider at one time during a single conversation or task.
8. **Fine-Tuning**: The process of taking a pre-trained general AI model and training it further on a smaller, specialized dataset so it performs better on a specific task (e.g., coding in Java).
9. **Zero-Shot Learning**: When an AI model successfully performs a task it was not explicitly trained to do, just by understanding the prompt without needing any examples.
10. **Embedding**: A way of translating text or words into lists of numbers (vectors) so that a computer can calculate the mathematical similarity or relationship between different concepts.
